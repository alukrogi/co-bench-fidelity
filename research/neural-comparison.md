# What Table 2 establishes about neural solvers

**At TSP-1000, the choice of DIMES decoder reverses the comparison with both CO-Bench agents.** The selected DIMES row has a longer tour than either agent. DIMES’s own MCTS-decoded result is shorter than both. The source also reports a shorter elapsed time for that result than the times listed for either agent, although the hardware and parallelism differ. Both CO-Bench versions describe selecting the best result per method and dataset. The table needs an eligibility rule to explain the decoder choice. ### Versions and units

The arXiv v3 table covers TSP at 500, 1,000 and 10,000 nodes, plus small and large Erdős–Rényi graphs for maximum independent set (MIS). The AAAI table drops TSP-500 and prints fewer digits. The preprint describes competitive performance at similar time budgets. The proceedings emphasize stronger MIS and weaker TSP results, and describe the agents as generally faster. Both tables report tour length (smaller is better) and independent-set size (larger is better), paired with a time or a missing entry. The minutes or hours in a cell describe elapsed computation. They do not specify a common per-instance cutoff. [arXiv v3, Table 2 and accompanying text](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.04310v3#Sx4.T2) · [AAAI, pp. 33131–33132](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/40596/44557)

### Which conclusions survive

For the displayed large-MIS rows, both agents report larger sets and shorter listed times than DIMES, the only neural comparator with a result there. On small MIS, Greedy Refine’s 42.35 exceeds all three listed neural sizes, while FunSearch’s 41.65 falls below DIMES’s 42.06. Timing order changes too. Greedy Refine’s 20.1 min is above DIMES’s 12.0 min on small MIS and below DIFUSCO’s 26.6 min. These are concrete, scope-limited comparisons between the reported operating points. [CO-Bench, arXiv v3 Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.04310v3#Sx4.T2)

Table 2 varies **instance size, solver and decoding policy**. A time-budget sweep would hold the instances and solver fixed, vary the allowed per-instance seconds, and record incumbent quality, failures and time to solution at each cutoff. Cross-source timings and missing cells cannot show whether a particular method wins at one, ten or sixty seconds, or how often exact optimization succeeds under those limits. A fair replication would publish paired instance IDs, an explicit decoder eligibility rule, per-instance quality and time, batch size, host/GPU resources and whether model training or agent development is counted separately.
